Home / Buyer guide

Buying your first home, one stop at a time.

New words, new costs, and a lot of paperwork. This guide explains each stage of buying a home in Dallas, what to expect, and what we handle for you.

The route

Six stops from your first budget to your front door. Most of our buyers spend a few months searching, then about 30 to 45 days getting from accepted offer to closing.

Have a question along the way? Ask our homebuyer assistant.

  1. Stop 1

    Know your budget

    Before you look at homes, look at your money. Check your credit report, add up your monthly debts, and decide what payment feels comfortable, not just what a lender might approve. Plan for more than the down payment: closing costs, moving, and a cushion for repairs.

    Good to know: Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are higher than in many states. Our payment calculator includes them.

  2. Stop 2

    Get preapproved

    A preapproval letter tells you your real price range and shows sellers you're serious. Compare offers from at least two or three lenders. A small difference in interest rate can change your monthly payment and what you pay over the life of the loan.

  3. Stop 3

    Pick your part of town

    We'll turn your wish list into priorities: commute, schools, yard, bedrooms, and whether you want an HOA. Then we match those to areas that fit your budget, from Oak Cliff to Richardson, so your search stays focused.

  4. Stop 4

    Tour homes and make an offer

    On tours we point out what first-time buyers often miss, like roof age, foundation movement from North Texas clay soil, HVAC age, and drainage. When you find the one, we use recent nearby sales to price your offer and include protections like inspection and financing contingencies.

  5. Stop 5

    Inspection, appraisal, and loan approval

    Once your offer is accepted, an inspector checks the home and the lender orders an appraisal to confirm its value. If the inspection finds problems, we negotiate repairs or a credit. Avoid opening new credit cards or making big purchases while your loan is finalized.

  6. Last stop

    Closing day

    A few days before closing you'll get a Closing Disclosure with your final loan terms and costs. Compare it with the Loan Estimate you received earlier. After a final walk-through, you sign at the title company, pay your closing costs, and leave with your keys.

    Good to know: never wire closing money based on emailed instructions alone. Call your title company at a number you already trust to confirm.

What closing really costs

Closing costs are the fees to finalize your mortgage and transfer ownership. They're separate from your down payment and usually add up to a few percent of the price.

Your lender has to give you a Loan Estimate within three business days of your application, so nothing should surprise you at the table. Some fees can be negotiated, and sellers sometimes agree to pay part of them. Ask us about adding a seller credit to your offer.

Help you may qualify for

  • FHA loans are government-insured mortgages with low down payment options, popular with first-time buyers.
  • Conventional loans can allow small down payments for qualified first-time buyers, with PMI until you reach about 20% equity.
  • VA and USDA loans can offer zero-down options for eligible veterans and buyers in eligible areas.
  • Down payment assistance is offered by some Texas and local programs. Ask us which ones fit your situation.
  • Free housing counseling is available from HUD-approved agencies at hud.gov/housingcounseling or 1-800-569-4287.

Questions first-time buyers ask us

Do I really need 20% down?

No. Many buyers put down much less. A smaller down payment usually means a higher monthly payment and, on conventional loans, private mortgage insurance (PMI) until you reach about 20% equity. The right amount depends on your savings and how much cushion you want to keep.

How long does buying a home take?

Searching can take a few weeks or a few months. After your offer is accepted, closing usually takes about 30 to 45 days while the inspection, appraisal, and loan approval are completed.

What's the difference between prequalified and preapproved?

Prequalification is a quick estimate based on what you tell a lender. Preapproval means the lender has checked your income, assets, and credit, which makes your offer much stronger.

Why are property taxes such a big deal in Texas?

Texas has no state income tax, so local governments rely heavily on property taxes. That makes them a large part of your monthly payment. If the home is your main residence, you can apply for a homestead exemption to lower your tax bill.
